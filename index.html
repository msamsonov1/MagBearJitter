Mathematical Physics & State Space Synthesis

The MagBearJitter sentinel addresses the fundamental trade-off in Active Magnetic Bearing (AMB) momentum flywheels deployed on astronomical spacecraft and lunar observatories. Mechanical mass eccentricity causes rotational synchronous forces, while geometric core runout introduces parasitic sensor errors. By dynamically mapping the acceleration-flux vector space $(\Phi, a)$, the software discriminates true rotor mass unbalance from sensor runout, injecting exact anti-phase cancellation currents to nullify chassis torque transmission.

1. AMB Maxwell Stress & Flux Linkage

For an 8-pole heteropolar active magnetic bearing, the total magnetic attractive force exerted on the ferromagnetic shaft in the radial degree of freedom $x$ is governed by Maxwell's stress tensor across dynamic air gaps:

$$F_{\text{mag}}(x, i) = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{4} \left[ \left(\frac{I_0 + i_x}{g_0 - x}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{I_0 - i_x}{g_0 + x}\right)^2 \right]$$

Where $\mu_0$ is vacuum permeability ($4\pi \times 10^{-7}\,\text{H/m}$), $N$ is coil turn count, $A$ is pole projected area, $I_0$ is bias current, $i_x$ is dynamic control perturbation, and $g_0$ is nominal stator-rotor gap ($500\,\mu\text{m}$). Coil magnetic flux linkage $\Phi_x$ couples directly to current and instant displacement:

$$\Phi_x(t) = \frac{\mu_0 N A}{2} \left( \frac{I_0 + i_x(t)}{g_0 - x(t)} \right) \approx \Phi_0 + k_i i_x(t) + k_x x(t)$$
Flux displacement sensitivity: $k_x = \frac{\Phi_0}{g_0} \approx 4.8 \times 10^3\,\text{Wb/m}$.

2. Centrifugal Mass Eccentricity & Micro-g Jitter

A rotating flywheel rotor of mass $m_r$ spinning at angular frequency $\Omega = 2\pi f_r$ with a center-of-mass eccentricity offset $e$ produces synchronous centrifugal force $F_c$ and angular runout torque:

$$F_c(t) = m_r \Omega^2 e \cos(\Omega t + \beta) \implies a_{\text{chassis}}(t) = \frac{m_r \Omega^2 e}{M_{\text{obs}}} \sin(\Omega t)$$

When transmitted into the lunar astronomical telescope chassis (mass $M_{\text{obs}}$), this dynamic unbalance produces structural acceleration $a(t)$ reaching $10^{-4}\,g$ without active damping, blowing past the strict micro-vibration pointing budget ($a_{\text{allow}} \le 0.1\,\mu g$ for diffraction-limited imaging).

$$Jitter_{\text{RMS}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T [a_x(t)^2 + a_y(t)^2] dt} \propto \frac{e \cdot \Omega^2}{M_{\text{obs}}}$$
At $12,000\,\text{RPM}$ ($\Omega = 1256\,\text{rad/s}$), an eccentricity $e = 1\,\mu\text{m}$ imparts over $7.8\,\text{N}$ of uncompensated synchronous shear.

3. Adaptive Inverse Current Injection Sentinel

The attitude control processor extracts the synchronous Fourier coefficients of dynamic flux ripple $\Phi(t)$ and radial acceleration $a(t)$ across the instantaneous rotor frame angle $\theta(t) = \int \Omega dt$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} C_k \\ S_k \end{bmatrix} = \frac{2}{T_r} \int_{0}^{T_r} \begin{bmatrix} \cos(k \Omega t) \\ \sin(k \Omega t) \end{bmatrix} \Phi(t)\, dt$$

The observer calculates the optimal counter-harmonic control injection $i_{\text{inj}}(t)$ via an adaptive LMS/Luenberger state-space estimator, allowing the rotor to spin freely around its inertial principal mass axis rather than the geometric stator center:

$$i_{\text{inj}}(t) = - \sum_{k=1}^3 \hat{W}_k(t) \cdot \left[ C_k \cos(k \Omega t) + S_k \sin(k \Omega t) \right]$$
Automatic Zero-Current Control (ZCC) eliminates reaction forces transmitted through magnetic flux gradients.

4. Physical Runout vs Mass Imbalance Inversion

A critical physical trap in magnetic bearings is sensor target runout (machining out-of-roundness $\delta_r(\theta)$ and electrical runout $\Delta\mu_r$). Legacy controllers mistake sensor runout for actual shaft motion, erroneously commanding coil currents that generate physical jitter.

$$x_{\text{sensor}}(t) = x_{\text{mech}}(t) + \delta_r(\theta) + \eta_{\text{thermal}}(t)$$

MagBearJitter separates electrical sensor runout from true mechanical acceleration by observing the quadrature phase correlation between coil terminal flux linkage $\Phi(t)$ and chassis piezoelectric micro-g sensor signals $a(t)$:

$$\mathcal{D}_{\text{runout}} = \oint \Phi(t)\, da(t) = \int_0^{T_r} \Phi(t) \frac{da}{dt} dt \implies \begin{cases} > \Gamma_{\text{th}}, & \text{Mass Imbalance} \\ \approx 0, & \text{Machined Runout} \end{cases}$$
Real-time trajectory integral $\mathcal{D}_{\text{runout}}$ prevents false bearing actuator excitation.
